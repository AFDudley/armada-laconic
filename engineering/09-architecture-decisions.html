ADR-0001

Adopt arc42 for engineering documentation · accepted · 2026-09-04

Context. A single mutable plan document was carrying four responsibilities at once — system decomposition, work breakdown, decision log, and schedule — and had been re-cut repeatedly, from workstreams to tiers to a tier/line matrix. The churn stemmed from the absence of separate, single-purpose artifacts and a stable "one model, many views" discipline.

Decision.

  • D1.1 Adopt arc42 (Starke/Hruschka) as the single documentation template.
  • D1.2 The system uses one item model (the §5 registry), and tiers and lines become views/tags rather than competing hierarchies.
  • D1.3 Decisions live here as ADRs, cross-cutting concerns in §8, and risks in §11.

Consequences. All engineering docs conform to the 12 arc42 sections. Building-block detail stays in the T0..T6 docs (§5), and "lines" are expressed through solution strategy (§4) and cross-cutting concepts (§8) rather than a parallel file tree.

Alternatives. C4/Simon Brown is diagram-centric and light on decisions and cross-cutting concerns; Views-and-Beyond/SEI is rigorous but heavyweight; and a bespoke structure is the status quo that failed.

ADR-0002

Deploy our own Railgun pool (don't rent Railgun's) · accepted · 2026-09-04

Context. We need a shielded pool with our own fee policy, plus the freedom to attach a settlement contract. The Railgun design can be reimplemented under our own control.

Decision.

  • D2.1 Stand up our own pool contracts and circuits, matching the Railgun design, under our own control (T0.0), with our own fee policy, our token allow-list, and a reserved deposit/payout hook. armada-poc built this pool: PrivacyPool routes to four modules by delegatecall, with no proxy. The deployed shield fee is 50 bps (scripts/deploy_privacy_pool.ts:264-265), not fee = 0; the contract default is 0 bps and the owner can raise it to the MAX_SHIELD_FEE_BPS cap of 1000 (10%).

Consequences. We own upgrades and audit, and revenue comes from the shield fee plus yield-adapter fees (ArmadaFeeModule) rather than a pool skim set to zero. We do not modify Railgun's deployed protocol or touch their live pool. Railgun users move funds in through a wallet action (ADR-0006).

Alternatives. Renting Railgun's live pool — which carries their ~0.25% fee, no fee control, and no settlement hook — was rejected.

ADR-0003

Keep Groth16; reuse Perpetual PoT Phase-1 + run our own Phase-2 · accepted · 2026-09-04

Context. Groth16 requires a per-circuit trusted setup. Two questions remained open: whether to run our own trusted setup, and whether to switch to Halo2 to avoid setup altogether.

Decision.

  • D3.1 Keep Groth16.
  • D3.2 Reuse the community Perpetual Powers of Tau for Phase-1, and run our own Phase-2 MPC over our circuit set.
  • D3.3 Security rests on a 1-of-N honest assumption, backed by a published transcript and a final randomness beacon (T0.1). The Phase-2 ceremony is open, not finished: the v1.0.0-ceremony tag's manifest lists every shape "status": "open" with no beacon, and main has only scripts/setup-dev.sh, which the repo marks unsafe for anything but local testing.
  • D3.4 Phase-2 is re-run only when circuits change — for example, the ADR-0005 fork-lite upgrade, or the selective-disclosure circuit (ADR-0020), which adds shapes to this same ceremony rather than starting a new one.

Consequences. There is no universal ceremony, since the expensive and risky part is inherited and vetted. Each circuit-set change costs a few days of MPC, and we take on no dependence on Railgun's specific ceremony instance. On-chain verification and mobile prover performance remain Groth16-cheap.

Alternatives. Reusing Railgun's published Phase-2 zkeys was rejected because it trusts their instance. Halo2/transparent setups were rejected because they carry worse mobile prover performance and on-chain gas, and would require a fresh audit, all to remove a soundness-only 1-of-N assumption.

ADR-0004

Settle via nitro-on-railgun, not RelayAdapt · accepted · 2026-09-04

Context. We need optimistic exits and arbitrary off-chain settlement games over shielded value. Railgun's native path is RelayAdapt recipes, which are governance-gated on-chain call composition.

Decision.

  • D4.1 Build the deposit/payout boundary (T0.3) that funds a go-nitro channel from a shielded note and settles the channel outcome back into fresh notes: notes in → normal Nitro → notes out.
  • D4.2 The Nitro game analysis stays isolated from the Railgun privacy analysis; the two meet only at the allocation→note boundary.

Consequences. We gain optimistic exits and arbitrary ForceMove games with no new cryptography and without governance-gated adapters. armada-nitro built this: ArmadaNitroAdjudicator carries the boundary inside the adjudicator contract itself, rather than as a second contract. depositFromNote funds a channel from a note, and a SHIELD-type allocation in _executeSingleAssetExit pays a note back out. There is no separate T0.3 contract and no fundChannel. RelayAdapt is not used anywhere in this codebase; no venue hedge leg (T4.2) exists yet.

Alternatives. RelayAdapt-only settlement was rejected: it is governance-gated, less expressive, and offers no channel exits.

ADR-0005

Amount privacy = Design A default; fork-lite deferred · accepted · 2026-09-04

Context. In the base construction, amounts are public at the deposit/payout boundary and on force-close. Hiding amounts in-play requires a circuit change.

Decision.

  • D5.1 Ship Design A, in which amounts are public only at the transparent boundary, as the default.
  • D5.2 Native channelized commitment ("fork-lite", T0.6), which provides amount-privacy-during-play, is an optional Phase-4 upgrade rather than part of the v1 spine.
  • D5.3 Fork-full — shielded ForceMove, research-grade — is excluded unless separately greenlit.

Consequences. v1 needs zero new crypto. Fork-lite, if greenlit, changes circuits and therefore re-runs Phase-2 (ADR-0003), requires a fresh audit, and imposes heavier mobile proving.

Alternatives. Fork-lite in v1 was rejected: it is unneeded for the feature set and adds ceremony, audit, and prover cost. Fork-full is out of scope.

ADR-0006

Grow the anonymity set with our own users; wallet move from Railgun · accepted · 2026-09-04

Context. A fresh pool's anonymity set starts near zero, which is the biggest non-engineering risk. Railgun users hold value that they may want to move into our pool.

Decision.

  • D6.1 Bootstrap our own crowd with new users, through incentives, our own market-making capital, and an LP flywheel (T0.7).
  • D6.2 The wallet makes it easy to move funds from Railgun (T6.7). The move is a Railgun unshield followed by a shield into our pool. It is a wallet feature, not a protocol component: no contract of ours reads or touches Railgun's pool.

Consequences. Privacy grows only with our own volume. Moved value joins our crowd like any other deposit. The move is a public step: the token, the amount and the time are visible on both pools, so an observer can match the two sides. A user who moves from Railgun's larger crowd into our smaller one has weaker privacy until our pool grows. The wallet states these costs before the user confirms (T6.7).

Alternatives. Renting Railgun's pool for its set was rejected in ADR-0002.

ADR-0007

Venue = RFQ posted-price v1; provably-fair CLOB is v2 · accepted · 2026-09-04

Context. Armada needs clearing, not a service-provider marketplace. Fair-ordering machinery is expensive.

Decision.

  • D7.1 The v1 venue is an RFQ posted-price dealer market (T4): Armada posts bid/ask on both sides on a take-it-or-leave-it basis and reposts if wrong, driven initially by a single priceSetter wallet that gives way to governance later.
  • D7.2 A provably-fair CLOB — a commit-reveal sequencer plus DSS, ex_net-grade — is a v2 per-venue opt-in (T3 + T4.6).

Consequences. A posted price has nothing to front-run, so v1 removes the entire ordering problem. ex_net becomes a special case — a DSS-federated venue that also proves fair ordering — layered on the same spine rather than a rewrite.

Alternatives. A CLOB/matcher in v1 was rejected: it is months of work requiring a DSS and sequencer, and it is not needed to clear.

ADR-0008

Transport = Waku pub/sub + libp2p-noise 1:1; optional Nym underlay · accepted · 2026-09-04

Context. The system needs both broadcast, for feed and quote discovery, and low-latency point-to-point exchange, for vouchers and quotes, running on servers, browsers, and phones.

Decision.

  • D8.1 Use Waku pub/sub for gossip and discovery, and libp2p-noise direct streams for the 1:1 hot loop (T2.3).
  • D8.2 This is one unified transport shared by every Armada service — watcher feeds, Nitro channels (including the cross-chain swap hubs and their DSS signing coordination), and the wallet — never a per-service bespoke transport.
  • D8.3 IP/metadata privacy is an optional Nym mixnet underlay: uniform, opt-in, off by default for latency and on for maximum privacy, sitting beneath the same transport everywhere.
  • D8.4 Mobile production uses native gomobile modules — go-waku plus go-libp2p — with an interim WebView browser-stack (T6.5).

Consequences. This reuses the transport Railgun's broadcaster network already speaks and unifies transport across all services, so no service (the swap hubs included) rolls its own. Noise provides encryption and peer-id authentication only — not IP privacy on its own; IP/metadata privacy is the optional Nym underlay (opt-in, uniform, latency cost), not a separate per-feature transport. Native mobile libp2p is unbuilt (§11).

Alternatives. gossipsub-only is worse for 1:1 latency. js-libp2p on React Native is not RN-compatible, which is why the native port exists.

ADR-0009

The proof path is the nimbus verified proxy, not a state-diff emitter · accepted · 2026-09-04; revised 2026-10-19

Context. A client on every target must verify Ethereum state against a header that a beacon light client checks: the execution header, storage values (rootHistory, nullifiers, the adjudicator's slots), the inclusion of one pool or armada-nitro log, and the completeness of a block's pool logs. The docs planned a nimbus-eth1 state-diff emitter mapped into IPLD, served by watcher-ts. status-im/nimbus-eth1 already ships a verified proxy for this purpose (labbook/2026-10-19-nimbus-verified-proxy-proofs.md).

Decision.

  • D9.1 Target the nimbus verified proxy as the proof path. It anchors headers to a beacon light client, verifies storage with EIP-1186 proofs, and proves a log by rebuilding the block's receipt trie (eth_getBlockReceipts), which also proves completeness of that block's logs. eth_getLogs ranges are not used for note sync, because an omitted log is undetectable in a range.
  • D9.2 The proxy runs natively on servers, as WASM in browsers, and as libverifproxy.a on mobile once a cross-compile exists (none exists upstream yet). watcher-ts becomes an optional, unverified server-side cache; a client re-verifies anything it reads from the cache through the proxy.
  • D9.3 The nimbus state-diff emitter, an IPLD mapper, and the cerc-io ipld-eth-server pipeline are not on the proof path. The pipeline is geth-only and dormant since August 2024, and its receipt data, in the current schema, carries no per-log proof to receiptsRoot.

Consequences. T1 shrinks from a state-diff emitter build to operating the proxy and patching it for the fixturenet's custom genesis. The client tier gains the WASM and mobile build cost instead. Historical eth_getProof still needs an archive execution node behind the proxy.

Alternatives. The nimbus-eth1 state-diff emitter with IPLD and a watcher-ts proof feed (rejected for the proof path: the proxy does the same verification upstream, with less new code). Reviving plugeth (rejected as stale). Bare JSON-RPC offers no proofs.

ADR-0010

Terminology conventions · accepted · 2026-09-04

Context. Several terms collided with established meanings or with Railgun/Ethereum vocabulary, causing repeated confusion.

Decision.

  • D10.1 Enforce:
  • deposit/payout contract for T0.3 — never "adapter."
  • RelayAdapt / Adapt modules / Cookbook only for Railgun's own value-moving recipe; Adapters (capital-A tier) only for Armada's T5.
  • boundary (or interface), never "seam" (collides with Feathers' testability term).
  • tier (T0–T6), never "layer" (collides with Ethereum L1/L2).
  • venue / clearing, never "marketplace."

Consequences. Docs are grep-clean for the banned terms, and cross-references use T#.# ids.

Alternatives. Ad-hoc wording is the status quo that caused the confusion.

ADR-0011

Market-making and LP-buffered USDC yield are out of v1 · accepted · 2026-09-05

Context. ADR-0007 fixed the v1 venue as a posted-price dealer market but left the dealer's operation implicit. An automated market-making solver — cross-venue internalization, flash-loan hedging, and Aave rebalancing (T4.2) — and the LP-buffered USDC-yield rail that rides it (T4.5) carry the venue's real operational complexity and risk: hedge MEV, LP cold-start, and the two timing clocks. None of it is needed to demonstrate private exchange and clearing.

Decision.

  • D11.1 v1 exchange is filled from a simple pre-funded venue inventory — an Armada-operated account holding both sides of a pair, filling at the posted price and topped up out-of-band.
  • D11.2 No automated market-making runs in v1: no internalization, no flash-loan hedge, no Aave rebalance loop. The venue solver (T4.2) and the LP-buffered USDC-yield rail (T4.5) move to v2.
  • D11.3 Consequently v1 yield is ETH/wstETH only (held as a note, T4.4), and USDC yield defers to v2.

Consequences. The net-new v1 venue surface shrinks to the posted-price contract (T4.0), the quote/settle app (T4.1), and the fee-split wiring (T4.3), all settling over reused go-nitro clearing. The yield→USDC swap still ships, filled from static inventory rather than a hedged maker. The hedge-MEV and LP-cold-start risks become v2 concerns (§11). v1 capacity is bounded by the static inventory and refilled manually; running dry means quotes are withdrawn, never a custody risk.

Alternatives. Ship the automated solver + LP rail in v1 (rejected — that is the market-making operation, the venue's hardest and riskiest part, and it is not required to clear). Drop exchange from v1 entirely (rejected — the yield→USDC swap is a v1 deliverable and works against static inventory).

ADR-0012

Delivery model: reuse-oriented incremental delivery; scopes as WBS work packages · accepted · 2026-09-05

Context. Requirements are given (the Armada docs), most components already exist (Railgun, go-nitro, watcher-ts, the wallet), and the key architecture choices are already made and frozen as ADRs 0002–0011. The remaining job is to specify and integrate incrementally without re-opening settled decisions or drifting across adjacent scopes. This is the classic shape of a reuse-oriented, requirements-given programme — not greenfield design, and not agile requirements-discovery.

Decision. Adopt an operating model with five rules, each drawn from established practice:

  • D12.1 Reuse-oriented specs (Sommerville reuse-oriented SE; CBSE). Each spec states interfaces + the delta (config + net-new glue) + acceptance, and cites reused components at pinned commits rather than re-documenting their internals.
  • D12.2 WBS of work packages (PMI, 100% rule; MECE). Decompose the programme into deliverable-oriented work packages — A nitro-on-railgun, B watcher parties, C yield, D client — each a system with a context boundary (arc42 §3) and an interface-control contract (ICD) to its siblings (INCOSE; ISO/IEC 15288). See 00-work-packages.md.
  • D12.3 Fixed architecture baseline. ADRs 0002–0011 are the baseline; specs build on them and never relitigate. Change means superseding an ADR, never editing a decided one.
  • D12.4 Traceability (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148). Every requirement traces up to an Armada requirement and down to a work package, and is written to the 29148 bar: unambiguous, verifiable, complete, traceable.
  • D12.5 Risk-first increments (Boehm spiral / Boehm–Lane ICSM; McConnell staged delivery). Deliver against the baseline in increments sequenced by risk; one walking skeleton retires integration risk first, then each work package deepens; the long poles (T1 ingestion, mobile transport, go-nitro maturity) get spikes first.

Consequences. Tiers (T0–T6) remain the shared building-block vocabulary (the product breakdown); work packages own subsets of tier-items and consume the rest across ICDs. The arc42 docs become per-work-package architecture descriptions plus a program context. Scope drift is structurally prevented: a work package may touch only its own boundary and its own deltas — everything else is a cited baseline decision or an external interface.

Alternatives. One monolithic plan across all tiers (rejected — it caused repeated re-cutting and scope drift). Pure agile backlog discovery (rejected — requirements are given, not to be discovered). Greenfield design docs (rejected — most components exist; the work is integration).

ADR-0013

Single engineering team; scope spans the whole Armada product; Laconic is prior art · accepted · 2026-09-05

Context. Earlier docs drew an Armada-builds-vs-Laconic-builds ownership boundary (§3; T5 marked "out of Laconic build scope"; the ADR-0012 / §0 WBS treated Armada's Adapters as external). That boundary no longer holds. This is one engineering team building Armada — a Railgun-based privacy product for USDC (confirmed by Armada's own thesis and docs: Railgun ZK circuits (BN254/Groth16), a shielded USDC pool, non-custodial). The team works from the ship-armada repos (armada-poc, armada-circuits, armada-nitro, armada-sdk, armada-client, armada-relayer, armada-interface), which already carry the pool, the circuits, the adjudicator, and the client SDK. Only go-nitro, vendored and then patched, is Laconic prior art still in the build; watcher-ts, ts-nitro, @cerc-io/peer, mobymask, laconic-wallet, and chain-signatures are not in any of the seven repos.

Decision.

  • D13.1 Scope both the Armada-side and Laconic-side tasks as one programme. The WBS covers the whole product, including what was "T5 Armada Adapters" (CCTP, Aave-v4 yield, Swaps) — now in-scope work, not external systems.
  • D13.2 The governing framing is "build Armada on Railgun, reusing Laconic prior art," not "Laconic supplies a substrate to Armada."

Consequences. §1 purpose reframes from Laconic work to satisfy Armada requirements to the team's work to build Armada, a Railgun-based product, on Laconic prior art. §3's build-ownership boundary becomes a reuse boundary (what already exists vs what is net-new), not an org boundary. T5's items enter the WBS; "external systems" shrinks to genuine third parties (Ethereum L1, Circle/CCTP, Aave, the Railgun design we reimplement, nimbus-eth1 upstream, the Waku network). The architecture baseline (ADRs 0002–0011) is unchanged — those technical choices stand.

Alternatives. Keep the two-org boundary (rejected — it's one team; the boundary created artificial "out of scope" gaps like T5 and mis-framed the yield/adapter work).

ADR-0014

Pool + circuits are our own implementation (net-new, audit-critical) · accepted · 2026-09-05

Context. The shielded pool and the Groth16 circuits are authored by the team rather than reused as third-party binaries: they carry our fee policy and the deposit/payout boundary, and we own their upgrade and audit. ADR-0002 (own pool) and ADR-0003 (own Phase-2) are executed as our own implementation of the Railgun design, not a binary reuse. armada-poc built the pool; armada-circuits built the Transact(N,M) circuit. go-nitro is reused, then patched (ADR-0004); Railgun's engine/cookbook served as a behavioral reference during the build.

Decision.

  • D14.1 The shielded-pool contracts (T0.0) and the Transact circuit (T0.1) are implemented by us — independently authored, spec-compatible with the Railgun design. Built: PrivacyPool and its four modules (armada-poc); Transact.circom and its 19 shapes (armada-circuits).
  • D14.2 The settlement rail reuses go-nitro, patched by six commits (ADR-0004); Railgun's engine/cookbook served as a reference, not as reused code; the community Perpetual PoT Phase-1 is reused and our own Phase-2 runs over our own circuits (ADR-0003 otherwise unchanged); Groth16 stands.

Consequences. T0.0 and T0.1 are net-new, audit-critical crypto-engineering — of a well-understood design, now built and tested (armada-poc, armada-circuits test suites). The "integration, not new cryptography" thesis (ADR-0012, §4) holds for the settlement rail (T0.2 adjudicator, which also carries the T0.3 deposit/payout boundary, plus T6.x) but not for the pool/circuits. T0.6 (native commitment) is a change to our own circuits, still not built. Note format may match Railgun, so that one wallet can hold both and the T6.7 move from Railgun is simpler, or diverge — our choice; our anonymity set is separate regardless.

Alternatives. Use Railgun's live deployed pool directly (rejected — no fee=0, no settlement hook; contradicts ADR-0002's rationale).

ADR-0015

Cross-chain shielded swap = armada-nitro channels + fronting hubs · accepted · 2026-09-10 · v1.5 (after v1, before the v2 research work)

Context. Users hold shielded value in an Armada pool on each chain and want to swap or move value across chains privately and self-custodially (general cross-chain, independent of CCTP). A naive design — unshield on X, bridge, shield on Y — produces a public matched pair (equal/related amount, bounded timing) that correlates the two legs and collapses the anonymity set. The audited go-nitro "nitro bridge" is a single-operator, L1-anchored mirrored-channel construction with a stubbed L2 adjudicator and no DSS — neither private nor trust-minimized as written (nitro-bridge-audit.md).

Decision.

  • D15.1 Clear cross-chain swaps as Nitro state channels funded and settled from shielded notes, routed through a small set of bonded cross-chain hubs that front from standing inventory. Key choices:
  • The pool is the privacy anchor, not the channel. Value enters already shielded, on fresh rotating keys, over the unified transport (ADR-0008, optional Nym underlay). Per-swap settlement is off-chain, so there is no per-user on-chain event to correlate — no Lightning-style routing privacy (onion routing / PTLC) is needed.
  • Fronting is the core mechanism. A hub with inventory on both chains gives the user the destination asset instantly from standing inventory; the user's input joins the hub's inventory; the hub squares its net cross-chain position later. Fronting supplies immediacy, the hub's spread, and the temporal decoupling that severs the per-user cross-chain link.
  • Atomicity via an off-chain hash-locked swap (Nitro's HashLockedSwap pattern; not PTLC): it makes the fronted advance atomic — the hub is guaranteed its inbound leg the instant the user receives the outbound. The hash lock is off-chain and visible only to counterparties already on both legs, so it is not a privacy leak here.
  • Hub rebalancing, inventory sizing, and pricing are out of protocol — operator business logic, at most a reference open-source daemon, never a wallet concern.
  • Hub trust is liveness-only, via user unilateral exit (ADR-0004 force-close, realized by the new adjudicator in ADR-0016).

Consequences. A net-new capability sequenced v1.5 — after v1, before the v2 matcher/ordering/bonded-federation work; a small (~10–12-point) increment on the finished v1 rail with no hard v2 dependency. Requires an Armada pool on every participating chain and depends on ADR-0016. Full amount-privacy on a contested force-close needs T0.6 (deferred, ADR-0005). Detailed construction in shielded-nitro-bridge-design.md.

Alternatives. Single-operator mirrored-channel bridge (rejected — trusted, stubbed L2, no privacy; the audited go-nitro bridge). Direct self-serve cross-chain (rejected — public matched pair, correlatable). PTLC / adaptor-signature atomic swaps (deferred — research-grade, and unnecessary because the pool, not the channel, carries privacy).

ADR-0016

armada-nitro adjudicator + DSS custodian/delegate (EVM + Nitro only) · accepted · 2026-09-10 · v1.5 capability

Context. Cross-chain shielded swaps (ADR-0015) need on-chain enforcement of channel outcomes on every participating chain, and each hub must be a threshold-key (DSS) principal, not a single EOA. Two audited facts constrain the design: go-nitro's ForceMove verifies state signatures by hard ECDSA ecrecover against fixed participant addresses — no pluggable verifier; and chain-signatures is a threshold Schnorr DSS. The laconic integration that would bind the DSS to Nitro is declared-but-unbuilt and CometBFT-coupled.

Decision.

  • D16.1 Build an armada-nitro adjudicator on each Armada chain — ForceMove's dispute machine plus: (a) note-native custody (funded by unshield-in, settled by shield-out; folds the T0.3 deposit/payout boundary in); (b) EIP-1271 contract-signature participants so a hub's stable custodian contract is the channel participant, with the custodian verifying its hot delegate ECDSA key internally and rotating it without changing the channel's FixedPart; (c) unilateral exit → re-shield on both chains.
  • D16.2 The DSS is EVM + Nitro only, no laconicd: the hub custodian (HubCustodian.sol) verifies a threshold signature on-chain with FROST over secp256k1 (KECCAK-256), via the frost-secp256k1-evm library (lib/frost-secp256k1-evm, pinned 65d03d0); signing coordination rides the unified transport (ADR-0008). This is not chain-signatures' Schnorr DSS or its kyber DKG: the built custodian verifies with FROST.verifySignature, and rotates its group key with FROST.isValidPublicKey, not a SchnorrSECP256K1.sol contract.
  • D16.3 The cold DSS group key gates funds, rotates the delegate, and (multi-entity) slashes; the hot delegate signs states.

Consequences. Two small net-new contracts (the adjudicator delta and the custodian) on top of reused ForceMove, threshold-Schnorr, and our own pool implementation — the weight is audit, not code. Deploying the adjudicator per chain gives each leg of the hash-locked swap real enforcement (fixing the audited L2-stub gap) and makes unilateral exit real, so hub trust stays liveness-only. Bonding/slashing (T0.4/T2.4) is the multi-entity economic layer (v2). Both DSS codebases are self-declared unaudited. Post-v1; depends on ADR-0015. Detail: shielded-nitro-bridge-design.md, nitro-bridge-audit.md.

Alternatives. Reuse the vanilla NitroAdjudicator + T0.3 (rejected — ECDSA-only, no custodian/DSS participant, cleartext outcomes). Finish the go-nitro L2 stub (rejected — not Railgun-aware, single-key, no enforcement). Have the adjudicator natively Schnorr-verify each state (rejected — heavier, and does not solve delegate rotation vs FixedPart immutability). Use laconicd's CometBFT distsig transport (rejected — EVM + Nitro only, no Cosmos dependency).

ADR-0017

Hub yield and swaps as channel state · accepted · 2026-10-11 · v1.5

Context. The first hub games (§14) are a WETH↔wstETH swap and Aave USDC lending. §14 sketches each as its own ForceMove app: a two-asset settlement app and a lending app that needs an on-chain rate reference for disputes. armada-nitro's E11 task already assumes a simpler shape ("option 2"), which no architecture document records. ADR-0011 defers USDC yield to v2.

Decision.

  • D17.1 On the armada-nitro rail (ADR-0016), yield-bearing assets are held in hub channels as non-rebasing share tokens (wstETH; waUSDC, Aave's ERC-4626 StataToken over the USDC reserve), and a swap is one co-signed reallocation of a channel state under ConsensusApp.
  • D17.2 Yield accrues to the share holder with no contract tracking interest.
  • D17.3 Channel amounts are token units; exchange rates are read off-chain at quote time and never on the settlement path.
  • D17.4 The user signs a swap state first and the hub countersigns, so the quoting hub holds the free option.
  • D17.5 The adjudicator gains an immutable asset allow-list, balance-delta accounting on deposit, and a reentrancy guard on every holdings-changing function; clients exit per asset when an all-assets transfer reverts. Invariants and requirements: 15.
  • D17.6 (supersedes D11.3) v1 yield is ETH/wstETH only (held as a note, T4.4). USDC yield arrives in v1.5 as waUSDC held in hub channels (D17.1); the LP-buffered USDC rail (T4.5) stays v2.

Consequences. USDC yield arrives with the v1.5 hub rail instead of v2, as a held asset rather than an LP-buffered rail (T4.5 stays v2). No new ForceMove app for the first games; the contract work is a hardening delta to the adjudicator. Borrowing needs liquidation and a price, so it stays a v2 app. The hub's Aave deposits and redemptions are its own inventory management, outside any channel, so Aave liquidity never conditions a user's exit. The fixturenet and E11 use Aave v3's StataToken; the Aave-v4 adapter of the work-package C plan would supply the same ERC-4626 shape.

Alternatives. One ForceMove app per game, as §14 first sketched (rejected for the first games: two net-new audited apps and an on-chain rate reference, for flows that need neither when both parties sign). Rebasing assets (stETH, aUSDC) as channel assets (rejected: balances drift away from fixed holdings, breaking per-asset solvency). A governed asset allow-list (deferred: keeps governance off the settlement path while the asset set is four tokens).

ADR-0018

Decisions are clause-level records compiled to a current view · accepted · 2026-10-11

Context. The ADR log is append-only and a decided ADR is never edited, so the current decision had to be reconstructed by replaying every later ADR that touched an earlier one — and nothing checked the replay. ADR-0011 still reads "USDC yield defers to v2" after ADR-0017 changed it; only its index row hinted otherwise. The remedy is to give each decision a clause ID, declare supersession on the new clause, and compile the live view. Style and mechanics — and the alternatives weighed — are recorded in decision-records.md.

Decision.

  • D18.1 (supersedes D12.3) The accepted ADRs remain the fixed architecture baseline; specs build on them and never relitigate, and a decided ADR is never edited. Each ADR's Decision is a list of numbered clauses (D<ADR>.<clause>); a change is a new ADR whose clauses supersede specific earlier clauses, declared on the new clause as (supersedes …) and restating whatever of the superseded clause still holds. The current decision is read from the compiled 09a-current-decisions.md — generated from this file by tools/compile-decisions.py, never hand-edited — not by replaying the log, and a pre-commit --check gate keeps that page and these clause/supersession rules consistent.

Consequences. The current spec is one generated page, correct by construction; authors write only new clauses and name what they replace, never editing old ADRs or hunting for every restatement. Status is one of {proposed · accepted · withdrawn}; only accepted ADRs contribute live clauses and only their supersessions apply, with a proposed ADR's supersessions reported as pending. Pointer prose ("amended by", "amends") leaves the index and status lines, since the (supersedes …) markers are the single source.

Alternatives. Editing ADRs in place with git as history (rejected — weakens the log and makes review a diff of decided text); a hand-maintained current-decisions page (rejected — an unchecked third copy that drifts); whole-ADR supersession (rejected — most changes replace one clause, not the whole decision). See decision-records.md.

ADR-0019

Architecture decisions state what must hold; code-level requirements live with the code · accepted · 2026-10-11

Context. ADR-0017's D17.5 and design page 15 listed requirements on armada-nitro's Solidity (an asset allow-list in the constructor, a balance-delta override of deposit, a reentrancy guard, a pragma pin). Those are implementation requirements for one component, recorded in the product's architecture log, away from the code and the tests that verify them. armada-nitro keeps its own task briefs (docs/tasks/), which is where a contract's requirements and acceptance tests belong.

Decision.

  • D19.1 Architecture decisions and design pages in this repo state what must hold (decisions and invariants). Requirements on how a component's code enforces them, and the tests that verify it, live in that component's repository and cite the invariants they implement.
  • D19.2 (supersedes D17.5) The armada-nitro adjudicator, with its clients and watchtowers, enforces invariants I1–I7 of 15; its asset allow-list is immutable. The contract-level requirements and their tests are armada-nitro's task E16.

Consequences. Page 15 keeps the invariants and the immutable-allow-list choice; armada-nitro's E16 brief holds the concrete requirements (S1–S6) and their tests, next to the code they constrain. A change to how a contract enforces an invariant is a change in that repo, not an ADR.

Alternatives. Keep code-level requirements in ADRs (rejected — they drift from the code, and the log fills with implementation detail no architecture reader needs). Move whole designs into component repos (rejected — the invariants span components: clients, watchtowers and the hub all depend on them).

ADR-0020

Selective disclosure of a chain of custody (voluntary, off-chain) · accepted · 2026-10-15

Context. A user who withdraws from the pool, for example to an exchange, can be asked where the value came from. Ownership of a public address is easy to prove with a signature. Inside the pool, no public record links a spend to the note that it spent, because a nullifier is Poseidon(nullifyingKey, leafIndex). To disclose the nullifyingKey would show every spend the wallet ever made. Value that goes through armada-nitro hub channels (ADR-0016, ADR-0017) adds a channel deposit and a payout to the chain. armada-poc/specs/GOVERNANCE.md §Pool access conditions permits voluntary disclosure tooling and forbids any disclosure as a condition of pool use. Design: 16.

Decision.

  • D20.1 Armada provides a voluntary, off-chain selective disclosure protocol. With it, a user proves a chain of custody for chosen value: from a shielding address, through notes, spends and armada-nitro channels, to a payout note or a withdrawal address. The user reveals nothing else about the wallet.
  • D20.2 A disclosure proof links a spend to the note it spent. The proof keeps the nullifyingKey and the viewing key secret. The protocol never asks the user to disclose either key.
  • D20.3 The spending key authorizes each disclosure statement inside the disclosure proof. Each public address in the chain signs the same statement, so the binding between the addresses and the shielded wallet holds in both directions.
  • D20.4 The protocol needs no change to the pool, its circuits or the armada-nitro contracts. It must never become a condition to deposit into, transfer in, or withdraw from the pool.
  • D20.5 A statement either claims an attribution for a channel's gain or claims none. A claimed attribution requires the channel's co-signed states, and the verifier checks all of them. Without a claim, the verifier reports only the deposit and payout amounts. The protocol has no optional evidence.
  • D20.6 The statement names one chain, one pool and, for channel hops, one armada-nitro adjudicator. The verifier accepts only events from that chain and those contracts.

Consequences. One net-new component: a small disclosure circuit (a few Poseidon hashes and one EdDSA check), with its own proof system choice and verifier. All other links in the chain use data that is already public or that the wallet already holds. Users do not need to rotate keys to limit what a disclosure reveals. A disclosure package can be forwarded and checked by anyone who holds it, and it opens the disclosed notes permanently. Address-ownership verification products, such as WalletConnect Wallet Verification, are complementary to this protocol and compatible with it.

Alternatives. Disclose the nullifyingKey (rejected: it reveals every spend of the wallet). Disclose the viewing key (rejected: it reveals every note and balance of the wallet). Rotate keys often to limit what one disclosure reveals (rejected as the mechanism: it does not hide spends made under a disclosed key, and each rotation costs a migration transaction and adds a step to every proof chain). A disclosure gate in the pool or the adjudicator (rejected: forbidden by §Pool access conditions). Optional attribution evidence (rejected: a package without it has no defined meaning, and a checker cannot be tested against it).
