A.7 — Anonymity-Set Strategy

work package A · reuse-oriented spec · 2026-09-05 Parent: A.0 Owns: T0.7 (anonymity-set strategy — bootstrap the crowd with new users)

A.7.1 Goal

Make the shielded value that A settles actually private in practice, not merely private in principle. The base construction (A.0.1) hides sender, recipient, and amount in-circuit; what it cannot manufacture is the crowd those hidden transactions blend into. A fresh pool starts with an anonymity set near zero, which ADR-0006 records as the biggest non-engineering risk to A. T0.7 is therefore a strategy item (per §5, status "strategy", not net-new crypto). It specifies the base shielded-payments primitive A delivers, the cold-start bootstrap plan, and the privacy metrics that show whether the crowd grows.

The crowd grows only from deposits into our own pool. Every note a spend can hide among is a note in our tree, so the only lever is to bring in users and volume.

A.7.2 Boundary

  • In scope. The shielded-payments primitive as a capability statement (it is inherited, not built, per A.7.3); bootstrap incentive and flywheel policy; and privacy metrics.
  • Out of scope. The pool contract itself and the changeFee/vkey levers; see A.2. The wallet move from Railgun, which is a wallet feature (T6.7, T6-client-apps.md). Native amount-privacy-in-play; see A.9. Selective disclosure, which is off-chain and gates nothing (ADR-0020, 16).
  • Phase-0 assumption. T0.7's bootstrap presumes a live pool (T0.0, A.2) and ceremony (T0.1, A.3). The residual dependency is that those builds ship before the crowd carries real value. Until they do, A.7 is a design and fixturenet exercise only.

A.7.4 Net-new delta (what A actually builds)

T0.7 is policy, not contract code. One delta:

  1. Bootstrap incentives and LP flywheel. Per ADR-0006, seed our own crowd with new users, through incentives, our own market-making capital, and an LP flywheel, so that shield and transact volume compounds the effective anonymity set. Early transactions have a small, weak set; the strategy's job is to grow it fast enough that effective-k crosses a usable threshold before real value depends on it. This is program and economic policy, not contract code, and A.7 owns the specification, not the treasury.

Value that a user moves in from Railgun through the wallet (T6.7) is an ordinary deposit. It counts towards the crowd only from the moment it is shielded here, like any other deposit.

A.7.5 Interfaces (ICD)

T0.7 exposes no on-chain interface. It consumes the pool's commitment and nullifier events (A.1.1 events, indexed by T1.2) to compute its metrics.

A.7.6 Acceptance / verification

  1. Shielded-payments primitive (inherited-capability check). On the fixturenet, a transact() with unshield=NONE moves value between two of our notes; assert the public transcript exposes only merkleRoot/boundParamsHash/nullifiers[]/output-commitment hashes, and no sender, recipient, or amount (A.1.7). This confirms A delivers shielded payments without any A-net-new code.
  2. Privacy metrics (the strategy's real KPI). Instrument and report: - Anonymity-set size — cardinality of live commitments in our accumulator (A.1.1 Commitments.sol). - Effective-k — the realistic same-denomination/recent-activity crowd a given transaction blends into (not raw set size; volume-weighted per ADR-0006 "grows only with our own volume"). - Boundary-exposure — count and volume of public boundary steps (shield, unshield, and deposit/payout per ADR-0005), since these are where amounts are transparent and are what the bootstrap trades against.

Acceptance means these three are computed and trend-visible on the fixturenet; the bootstrap target is effective-k rising with volume, not a fixed one-shot number.

A.7.7 Risks / open

  • Cold-start weakness window. Until the bootstrap flywheel lifts effective-k, early users have a small crowd, and large or oddly-denominated transactions are self-deanonymizing. The mitigation is policy: incentivize volume and encourage common amounts. There is no engineering fix in v1.
  • Boundary-exposure versus liquidity tension. Each shield, unshield, and deposit/payout is a transparent-amount event (ADR-0005); more deposits raise boundary-exposure even as they raise set size. The metric suite (A.7.6.2) exists to keep this trade visible.
