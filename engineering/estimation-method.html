Estimation method: points and 5090 hours

estimation method · 2026-10-20

This is the estimation method for armada-laconic. The plan (build-plan.md) states every unit of work in these points and converts them to hours on the 5090 with these rules. It replaces the old "linear difficulty points" (1–10) scale; that scale and its numbers are gone from this doc set.

Source: session 01a150cb, 2026-10-19 03:14 to 03:31 UTC, recorded in labbook/2026-10-18-contract-owned-notes-and-zk-channel-apps.md §2 ("Context scale"), §7 and §8.

1. The unit

One point is 10,000 tokens of peak context that one worker must hold at one moment (exophial/src/exophial/estimate.py:199, TOKENS_PER_POINT = 10_000; exophial issue exo-231a.1). Points measure the size of a task, not its difficulty, and not cumulative token spend.

A chunk is one unit of work that one worker does in one session: one brief, one branch, one acceptance check. Every plan item is a chunk or a list of chunks.

2. Size limits

  • The 27B model on the 5090 holds 163,200 tokens, so the exophial dispatcher refuses any pebble above 16 points on the 5090. marks holds 262,144 tokens, so 26 points there.
  • A worker starts at about 86,000 tokens before its first tool call (one measured cloud worker, session 01a0fd64, 2026-10-02). On the 5090 that leaves about 8 points of real work.
  • So a chunk for the local route is at most 9 points. A larger chunk is split, or starts on Sonnet.
  • No chunk is above 12 points on any route.

Anchors from the MetaLeX plan: a bootstrap chunk is 4 points; a derivation with test vectors is 7; a funding flow with a privacy assertion is 9; a deploy module is 10.

3. From points to hours

  • Decode is not the limit. The 5090 decodes 45 tok/s on average under real agent load (vLLM lifetime metrics, session 01a0deb8, 2026-09-27), and 116.7 to 138 tok/s warm (~/qwen3.8-27b-vllm-omp-setup.md lines 216-242). One measured worker wrote about 1,100 output tokens per point (session 01a0fd64). On decode alone, 30 minutes covers about 70 points.
  • Turn overhead is the limit. A pilot pebble on marks took 923 s over 62 turns, about 15 s per turn, mostly tool execution, prefill and the gate (session 01a0bd0d, 2026-09-25).
  • Rule: one 30-minute session completes one chunk of about 10 points, or two chunks if both are small (about 5 points or less).
  • Hours for a plan = (number of chunks, counting two small chunks as one) × 0.5 hours, before rework. Example: the 209-point list has about 27 chunks, so about 27 sessions, or 13 to 14 hours of worker time.
  • Parallel hosts shorten the calendar time, not the worker hours. The dependency graph decides how many chunks can run at once.

4. How the plan uses it

  • Every stage lists its chunks. Each chunk has an id, a one-line deliverable, its dependencies, its points, and its route (5090 if at most 9 points; otherwise split, or Sonnet).
  • Each stage gives its total points, its session count and its hours by §3.
  • The plan ends with the totals: points, sessions, 5090 hours, and the critical path in sessions.
  • Circuit work and the critical-path chunks P1 and N5 carry a routing note: circom on the 27B model is not measured (lab book §8), so these are the chunks most likely to escalate to Sonnet through contextPromotion. Every chunk still starts on the 5090 (operator ruling, CHUNK-RULES.md rule 1); there is no separate plan-time Sonnet assignment.

5. Limits of the method

  • The output-per-point figure and the 15 s turn come from one sample each.
  • Rework, review and gate failures are not in the hours.
  • Mark any estimate that rests on these samples as [INFERENCE] where it matters.
